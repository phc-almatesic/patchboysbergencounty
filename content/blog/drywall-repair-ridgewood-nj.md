## Common Drywall Repair Problems in Ridgewood Homes

### Cracks from Seasonal Movement

Bergen County gets real seasons — hot, humid summers and cold, wet winters — and that constant expansion and contraction takes a toll on your walls over time. In older Ridgewood homes, you'll often see hairline cracks forming along ceiling seams, around doorframes, and in corners. These aren't usually structural. They're the result of wood framing and drywall reacting differently to temperature and humidity changes throughout the year. That said, if a crack keeps coming back or is wider than 1/4 inch, it's worth having someone take a look to rule out any underlying issues.

### Water Damage and Staining

Between heavy spring rains, aging roofs, and the occasional burst pipe, water intrusion is one of the most common reasons Ridgewood homeowners call us. Water damage doesn't just stain drywall — it can cause it to bubble, sag, and become soft and crumbly. The critical thing to understand here is that you need to fix the source of the water *first*. Once that's resolved, damaged drywall sections usually need to be cut out entirely and replaced rather than patched over. Painting over a water stain without proper treatment will just result in it bleeding through again.

### Nail Pops

If you're seeing small circular bumps or cracked paint dots appearing across your walls or ceiling, those are nail pops — and they're extremely common in homes built in the 1960s through 1980s, which covers a lot of Ridgewood's housing stock. When the lumber behind your drywall dries and shrinks over time, it can push the fasteners outward. The fix is straightforward but does require the right technique: you can't just hammer the nail back in and spackle over it, or it'll pop again within a year.

### Holes and Dings from Daily Life

This one doesn't need much explanation. Doorknobs, furniture moves, kids, renovation projects — holes happen. A small drywall repair from a doorknob punch-through is a relatively simple fix. Larger holes from removing old shelving, recessed lighting work, or plumbing access cuts require more skill to make invisible, especially when [texture matching](/services/texture-matching/) is involved.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can you match the existing texture on my walls?**

Yes — texture matching is one of the most requested (and most underestimated) parts of drywall repair. If your walls have an orange peel, knockdown, or any other texture, a patch that's smooth will stand out like a sore thumb. We have the tools and experience to replicate most common textures so the repair blends in. In cases where the original texture is very unusual or aged, we'll talk through options with you honestly before starting work.

**Q: How long does a typical drywall repair take?**

Most single repairs take two to four hours, including compound application and drying time. Larger jobs involving multiple repairs or water-damaged sections may take a full day. One thing to keep in mind: drywall compound needs to dry completely before painting. We'll let you know what to expect for your specific job.

**Q: Should I paint over my repair myself or hire someone?**

That's entirely up to you. We can leave the repair primed and ready for your painter, or we can handle the [painting](/services/painting/) ourselves. If you prefer to paint it yourself, we'll make sure the surface is properly primed so it accepts paint evenly.

**Q: I have plaster walls in my older Ridgewood home — can you repair those too?**

Absolutely. A lot of homes in Ridgewood were built before drywall was standard, and they have original plaster walls that require a different repair approach. We offer [plaster repair](/services/plaster-repair/) as a dedicated service and have experience working with the key-and-lath systems found in many older Bergen County homes.
