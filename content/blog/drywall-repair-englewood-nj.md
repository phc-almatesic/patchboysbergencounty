## Common Drywall Repair Problems in Englewood Homes

### 1. Cracks From Seasonal Temperature Swings

Bergen County winters are no joke, and Englewood's older homes take a beating every year as temperatures swing from freezing to mild and back again. That constant expansion and contraction puts stress on drywall joints and seams, leading to the kind of hairline cracks you'll often find running along corners, above windows, or following the lines of the wall studs behind the drywall. While small cracks can sometimes be a cosmetic issue, larger or recurring ones can signal settling or structural movement worth having checked out.

### 2. Water Damage and Staining

Whether it's a slow roof leak, a bathroom pipe sweating behind the wall, or an HVAC condensation issue, water and drywall are a bad combination. Englewood's older housing stock — plenty of homes here date back to the early 1900s — means aging plumbing and roofing systems are common culprits. Water damage shows up as bubbling paint, soft or crumbling drywall, brown staining, or even mold growth in more serious cases. The key is to fix the source of the moisture first, then address the drywall damage properly rather than just painting over it.

### 3. Holes and Dents From Everyday Life

Door handles punching through walls. Furniture moving gone wrong. Kids. It happens in every house, and there's no shame in it — but those holes and dents don't fix themselves. Small nail holes from hanging pictures are easy enough to handle on your own, but anything larger than a couple of inches usually calls for proper patching with mesh, joint compound, and careful feathering to blend seamlessly with the surrounding wall. Done poorly, patches are immediately obvious under paint.

### 4. Popped Nails and Fastener Problems

In older Englewood homes, it's common to find drywall that was originally installed with nails rather than screws. Over time, those nails can work their way out of the studs, creating small raised bumps or "popped" spots on the wall surface. Left alone, these can eventually crack the drywall around them. The fix involves securing the area properly and re-finishing the surface — not just hammering the nail back in and painting over it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does a typical drywall repair take?**

Most standard repairs — patches, cracks, small holes — can be completed in a single visit that lasts anywhere from one to three hours. Larger jobs involving water damage or multiple areas may require a follow-up visit for a second coat of compound once the first has fully dried. We'll give you a realistic timeline when we assess the job.

**Can you match my existing wall texture?**

Yes, texture matching is one of our core services. Whether your walls have a light orange peel finish, a heavier knockdown texture, or smooth walls, we work to match it as closely as possible. Perfect matching depends on the age and condition of the existing texture, but our goal is always a repair that's invisible once painted.

**Do I need to fix the source of a leak before you repair the drywall?**

Absolutely — and we'll remind you of this if it's relevant to your situation. Repairing drywall over an active moisture problem is just a temporary fix. The new drywall will be damaged again within months. We'll let you know if we see signs of ongoing moisture during our estimate so you can address the root cause first.

**Do you work on ceilings as well as walls?**

Yes. Ceiling repairs are actually very common, especially in older Englewood homes where plaster ceilings have developed cracks or where water damage has compromised the ceiling drywall. Ceilings require a bit more care in finishing and texture matching, but it's work we do regularly. Learn more about our [ceiling repair services](/services/ceiling-repair/).
