## Plaster Repair Cost in Bergen County, NJ (2026 Guide)

If you've got cracked, crumbling, or water-damaged plaster walls in your home, the first question on your mind is probably: *how much is this going to cost me?* The honest answer is that plaster repair costs in Bergen County, NJ typically range from **$150 to $1,500+**, depending on the size and severity of the damage, the type of repair needed, and the condition of the surrounding plaster.

For small hairline cracks or minor patches, you're usually looking at the lower end of that range. Larger areas of damage — think sagging ceilings, extensive water staining, or sections where the plaster has separated from the lath — will push costs higher. Below, we break down what you can realistically expect to pay in 2026, and what factors drive the price up or down.

## What Affects the Cost?

### 1. Size of the Damaged Area
This is the most straightforward factor. A hairline crack that runs along a corner seam takes far less time and material than a two-square-foot section of plaster that has blown out from the wall. Most contractors price plaster repair by the square foot or by the job scope.

### 2. Type and Severity of Damage
Surface cracks are relatively simple. But if the underlying lath is rotted, if the plaster keys have failed (causing the plaster to pull away from the wall), or if water damage has soaked through multiple layers, the repair becomes more involved. A contractor needs to address the root cause — not just patch over the symptom.

### 3. Accessibility and Location
Repairs on flat walls are straightforward. Ceilings are harder. High ceilings, cathedral ceilings, or areas that require scaffolding or specialized equipment add time and cost. If you're in a home in [Paramus](/areas/paramus/) or [Teaneck](/areas/teaneck/) with high ornate ceilings, expect that to be reflected in the estimate.

### 4. Texture Matching
Older plaster often has a distinct texture — skip trowel, sand finish, orange peel, or completely smooth — and getting a patch to blend seamlessly takes real skill. If your walls have a unique or aged texture, [texture matching](/services/texture-matching/) adds time to the job and affects the final price. A patch that doesn't match stands out like a sore thumb, so this step isn't one to skip.

### 5. Number of Coats Required
Traditional plaster repair often involves multiple coats — a scratch coat, a brown coat, and a finish coat — allowed to dry properly between applications. That process takes longer than drywall repair and requires specific materials. Shortcuts here lead to cracks reappearing within months.

### 6. Whether Painting Is Included
Once the plaster is repaired and textured, it typically needs to be primed and painted. Some contractors include this; others don't. Clarify this upfront. If you need painting work done as part of the project, our [painting services](/services/painting/) can handle that as well, so the finished result looks complete — not patched.

## Is This Covered by Insurance?

Sometimes, yes — but it depends on the cause of the damage.

**Homeowners insurance typically covers plaster repair when the damage is caused by:**
- A sudden pipe burst or accidental water leak
- Storm damage (wind, hail, falling trees)
- Fire or smoke damage

**Insurance generally does NOT cover:**
- Gradual water damage or slow leaks (considered a maintenance issue)
- Normal aging, settling cracks, or wear and tear
- Damage resulting from neglected maintenance

If your plaster was damaged by an insured event, document the damage thoroughly with photos before any repairs begin, and contact your insurance provider to file a claim. ## How to Get the Best Value

### Get a Proper In-Person Estimate
Plaster repair is not easy to quote over the phone or from a photo. There's too much that can't be seen without physically assessing the wall — how far the damage extends, whether the lath is compromised, what finish is needed. Always request an in-person estimate.

### Don't Wait on Water Damage
If moisture is involved, time matters. Water-damaged plaster that's left alone can lead to mold growth and structural deterioration behind the wall. What starts as a $400 repair can become a $1,500 project if ignored for a few months.

### Hire Someone Who Knows Plaster
Drywall and plaster are different materials with different repair techniques. Make sure whoever you hire has real experience with plaster — not just drywall patching. Ask to see examples of previous plaster work, especially texture matching on older homes.

### Bundle Repairs When Possible
If you have multiple areas of damage throughout the house, getting them all addressed in one visit is almost always more cost-effective than scheduling separate jobs. Contractors can work more efficiently, and you save on mobilization time.

### Ask What's Included
Clarify whether the quote includes priming, painting, cleanup, and any necessary exploratory work (like checking what's behind a damaged section). A low quote that excludes key steps isn't really a low price.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is plaster repair more expensive than drywall repair?**
A: Generally, yes. Plaster repair requires specialized materials, multiple coats, and longer drying times between applications. It also demands more skill, particularly when texture matching is involved. That said, the results on an older home look far better than simply cutting out plaster and installing a drywall patch — which can look obvious and affect resale value.

**Q: How long does plaster repair take?**
A: Small patches can often be completed in a single visit, but multi-coat plaster repairs may require two visits to allow proper drying time between coats. Water-damaged areas may need additional time to fully dry out before repairs can begin.

**Q: Can I repair plaster myself to save money?**
A: Small hairline cracks can be filled with a flexible joint compound as a temporary fix. But for anything involving structural damage, failing plaster keys, or texture matching, DIY attempts often make the problem worse — or create a patch that's visually worse than the original damage. Professional repairs are worth the investment for results that last.

**Q: Does the age of my home affect the cost?**
A: It can. Homes built before the 1950s often have three-coat plaster over wood lath, which requires more careful assessment and repair than newer single-coat plaster. The materials and techniques need to be matched to the original construction for the repair to hold properly over time.
